About

Swastik P.

I made my first dollar online at 14 selling Minecraft mods. I still remember the rush I felt as my crypto wallet balance went up. Thought I'd figured it out.

Then I lost all of it because I stored the earnings in crypto, I couldn't really withdraw it since I was 14. Watched my balance hit zero. I also rememember that feeling. Didn't even have the stomach to be mad about it. Just stared at the screen for a while.

That's when I first realized selling isn't the hard part. Keeping the money is.

Money was tight growing up. Not to tell a sob story, but it was the reason I started looking at ways to make money online. And trust me I tried everything. I felt like if I tried everything, something had to work.

Around 15, while in a niche make-money-online community, I met someone who had actually achieved what I wanted. A 19-year-old coach making real money. Not theory. Actual cash.

I immediately started to learn everything I could from him. Working with him showed me how the coaching space actually works. Not the guru version. The mechanics. The hard shit nobody talks about.

That's also when I made a few friends, who just so happened to be aspiring coaches. And almost accidentally, I started to help them with their businesses with the knowledge I had gained. We'd hop on calls at 2 AM. I helped them structure offers, write their first sales messages, close their first clients.

They were the ones who told me I had a knack for this. That I should take it seriously.

So I did. I worked with coaches one-on-one. Studied launch cycles, sales funnels, retention systems. Got so obsessed I forgot I still was in high-school. My grades tanked. Went from top of my class to barely passing.

Not proud of that. But just to tell you about how deep I went.

Three years later. I've graduated. I know what works because I've been doing it since I was old enough to need my parents' permission for most accounts.

Why I built this

Here's the thing I kept noticing.

Coaches who were great at their craft kept burning out. They'd land clients, deliver results, then hit a wall. The launches stopped working. The calendar filled up with calls they couldn't take. Revenue dropped to zero and they'd start from scratch.

Every time.

Same problem. Different person.

The businesses that actually scaled — the ones past $50k a month — had one thing in common. They built systems. Systems for getting leads. Converting them. Delivering results without the founder doing every call. Keeping clients around longer.

Not luck. Not a bigger audience. Infrastructure.

The Kreator Syndicate is just that. A structured way to build what actually works. No fluff. Eight operators per quarter because attention is the constraint and I'm not willing to let that slide.
